## Executive Summary
This study estimates the loss of storage capacity of the Wadi Majlas dam reservoir due to sediment deposition, and derives the revised Elevation–Area–Volume (EAV) curve after sedimentation. The analysis follows the United States Bureau of Reclamation (USBR) Erosion and Sedimentation Manual, Chapter 2, Section 2.6, comprising reservoir trap efficiency (§2.6.1), density of deposited sediment (§2.6.2) and sediment distribution within the reservoir by the Empirical Area-Reduction Method (§2.6.3).
The reservoir (74.6 m high dam, full supply level 83 m a.s.l., wadi bed 19.4 m, original capacity 94.00 Mm³ at FSL) is classified as Type II (flood-plain/foothill; shape factor m = 2.90). Its 571.3 km² catchment delivers a sediment yield of 122 m³/km²/yr. Catchment topsoil texture from ISRIC SoilGrids is 18% clay, 27% silt and 55% sand, giving a 50-year average deposited unit weight of 1304 kg/m³ (operation 2 – moderate to considerable drawdown).
Headline results are summarised below; a full comparison is given in the Results section.

Over 50 years the reservoir is projected to lose 3.485 Mm³ (3.7%) of its storage at full supply level, and the effective reservoir bed at the dam rises from 19.4 m to 23.1 m a.s.l. Over 100 years the loss is 6.969 Mm³ (7.4%).

## Introduction
The Wadi Majlas dam is a flood-protection structure in Wilayat Quriyat, Sultanate of Oman, with provision for water retention. All reservoirs impounded by dams trap a portion of the sediment delivered by their catchments; over time this deposition reduces storage capacity and raises the reservoir bed, affecting flood-routing performance, the useful life of any conservation storage and the low-level outlet works.
The objectives of this study are to:
- estimate the volume of sediment that will deposit in the reservoir over 50- and 100-year horizons;
- characterise the deposited sediment density from catchment soil texture;
- distribute the deposited sediment through the reservoir by depth; and
- derive the revised Elevation–Area–Volume curve after sedimentation.

## Methodology
The analysis follows the USBR Erosion and Sedimentation Manual, Chapter 2, Section 2.6. The three components are trap efficiency, deposited density and sediment distribution.

### Reservoir sediment trap efficiency (§2.6.1)
Trap efficiency (TE) is the fraction of the inflowing sediment retained in the reservoir. For large storage reservoirs the manual recommends Brune's (1953) median curve, in which TE is a function of the ratio of reservoir capacity to mean annual inflow. In the absence of a mean-annual-inflow estimate, and because Majlas is a large reservoir relative to its sediment supply, a trap efficiency of 100 % is adopted here at the client's instruction (a conservative assumption for storage loss). ## Discussion and Limitations
Three modelling choices should be revisited as better data become available:
- Trap efficiency was set to 100%. To apply Brune's median curve, the mean annual inflow volume is required; the capacity/inflow ratio then gives TE. Mean annual inflow can be estimated as catchment area × mean annual runoff depth, where runoff depth is obtained from a rainfall–runoff model, a runoff coefficient applied to mean annual rainfall, or gauged records. For a large reservoir a TE close to 100% is expected, so the present storage-loss estimate is only mildly conservative.
- The sediment supply was provided as a volume (122 m³/km²/yr). The §2.6.2 deposited unit weight is reported and can rescale this volume if the yield's reference density is known (deposited volume = trapped volume × reference density / W_T); by default no rescale is applied and the given volume is treated as the deposited volume.
- Deposition above full supply level (in any flood pool) is neglected, which is conservative for storage below FSL. The delta deposit at the reservoir head (§2.6.4) is not modelled.

## Conclusions
Following USBR Section 2.6, the Wadi Majlas reservoir is projected to lose 3.485 Mm³ (3.7%) of its 94.0 Mm³ storage at full supply level over 50 years, and 6.969 Mm³ (7.4%) over 100 years. The effective reservoir bed at the dam rises to 23.1 m (50-year) and 26.3 m (100-year) a.s.l. The revised Elevation–Area–Volume curves derived here should be used for reservoir routing, storage-yield and outlet-works reviews.
